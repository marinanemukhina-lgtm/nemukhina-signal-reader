# Psychotherapy and motivational-interviewing adapter

Use this reference when resistance, ambivalence, motivation, alliance, relational strain, repair, or conversational influence may be changing the signal.

The purpose is not to diagnose or conduct psychotherapy. Transfer only the interactional reasoning principles.

## 1. Resistance is partly interactional

Do not model resistance as a fixed property of B.

Use:

`resistance_observed = f(state_B, relationship_state, wording_A, pressure_A, timing, context)`

A resistant response may indicate disagreement, fear, low trust, overload, reactance, misunderstanding, value conflict, or a response to how A is interacting.

Never infer a stable trait such as "difficult", "unmotivated", or "noncompliant" from one resistant episode.

## 2. Change talk and sustain talk are elicited evidence

Motivational interviewing shows that interviewer behavior is associated with what kind of language the client produces.

Therefore tag statements supporting change or the status quo as `ELICITED` when they arise in response to questioning.

Do not treat the mere production of change-oriented language as uncontaminated evidence of a pre-existing hidden state.

Track both:
- what the person says in favor of change;
- what the person says in favor of not changing;
- what interaction immediately preceded each.

## 3. Alliance is a measurement condition

The collaborative relationship affects disclosure, correction, participation, and willingness to remain in the interaction.

Treat alliance-like variables as part of `C_AB`, not as decorative rapport.

Useful components:
- agreement on purpose/goal;
- agreement on task/process;
- relational trust/bond;
- perceived autonomy and respect.

Poor alliance can reduce signal quality even when the hidden state itself is unchanged.

## 4. Rupture is data, not just failure

A rupture may appear as:
- withdrawal, minimal answers, disengagement, polite compliance;
- confrontation, disagreement, criticism, challenge;
- sudden topic shift or avoidance;
- drop in cooperation after a specific move by A.

When a rupture follows an intervention, consider whether the intervention caused the new signal.

Use sequence analysis:

`move_A -> rupture cue_B -> repair attempt_A -> response_B`

This sequence is more informative than the rupture cue alone.

## 5. Repair creates a discriminating probe

A repair attempt can help distinguish causes of resistance.

Low-contamination repair moves include:
- acknowledge tension explicitly but neutrally;
- invite correction of A's interpretation;
- restore choice/autonomy;
- clarify goal mismatch;
- ask what in the interaction is not working.

Then compare B's response. Improvement after repair is evidence that interactional factors mattered; it does not prove they were the only cause.

## 6. Preserve autonomy to reduce reactance

Pressure can create resistance that A later misreads as evidence about preferences.

Check whether B's response changes when autonomy is restored.

A useful diagnostic contrast is:

`pressure condition` vs `choice-preserving condition`

When safe and ethical, prefer the latter for inference.

## 7. Reflective verification

Before escalating interpretation, reflect the inferred meaning and ask for correction.

Use:

`observation -> tentative reflection -> correction/confirmation -> model update`

Treat confirmation after a highly leading reflection as weaker evidence than spontaneous self-description.

## 8. Therapist/interviewer behavior is part of provenance

For every important statement, preserve the preceding conversational move when possible.

Example evidence record:

`e_k = {statement_B, preceding_move_A, alliance_state, pressure_level, autonomy_level, time}`

This helps distinguish spontaneous state expression from interaction-produced speech.

## Transfer to general agent reasoning

- resistance -> interaction-dependent signal, not trait;
- change talk/sustain talk -> elicited evidence with provenance;
- alliance -> measurement condition affecting disclosure quality;
- rupture -> interaction-state transition;
- repair -> reversible discriminating intervention;
- autonomy support -> contamination reduction;
- reflective listening -> model-verification step.

## Research anchors

Use as prior-art anchors:
- Motivational Interviewing process research on MI-consistent behavior, change talk, and sustain talk;
- alliance rupture and repair work associated with Safran, Muran, Eubanks and later meta-analyses;
- current psychotherapy-alliance research emphasizing listening, collaboration, transparency, reliability, and shared decision-making.
